## MaceWard  (Mace & Ward (2002))
- conception: The artistic process begins with the conception of the artistic work. The work is introduced by an idea or a more or less vague impression. As Mace and Ward state, 'the genesis of an artwork arises from a complex context of art making, thinking, and ongoing experience.'
- development: During the development stage, artists structure, complete, and restructure the idea. They identify the work's development possibilities according to their ideas and feelings. Artists make implicit and explicit decisions by observing their work; this evaluation prompts them to question the ideas, expressions, metaphors, and analogies they wish to use — and those they prefer to abandon or put aside for future work. Trials, tests, and fiddling characterize this stage, allowing artists to form a more precise idea of the initial project.
- realization: At this phase the work undergoes a transformation from a purely conceptual entity into a conceptual and physical entity. The artwork now takes on physical constraints that may or may not have been previously considered or foreseen. As the concept of the work informs its physical structure, the process of physically making the artwork influences the development of the concept, so that content and form inform each other in an advancing process of development. Making an artwork does not involve rendering a prethought idea; it involves a process of negotiation between the artist and the developing work.
- finalization: Finalization is the resolution of the artistic work. The artist evaluates the production and decides that the work is finished — or alternatively, chooses to pursue it further, abandon it, postpone it, store it, or destroy it. If the artist considers the work successful and satisfying, they may exhibit it. Finalization implies a deliberate judgment about the work's completeness.
- none: sentence does not fit any of the above stages. Use freely when uncertain — do not force a label.
